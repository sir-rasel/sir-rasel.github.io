In February 2026, I completed **5 years** of my career. During these 5 years, I served responsibility for **4 different designations**. Started as a ***Software Engineer (Level 1)*** in March 2021. Next year, promoted to **Software Engineer (Level 2)**. Year after, joined as **Senior Software Engineer**, and currently an ***Assistant Lead Software Engineer*** after last promotion.

So I feel I should share my learnings (so far) from my career. Maybe freshers or juniors can benefit from it.

***Please note***: all the points below are my personal opinions, and it's completely fine to disagree. So let's start...

---

## Do's
- Learn programming and write lots of code during the first 3 years of your university life.
- Do problem solving and competitive programming in university life.
- Do a side project at the 4th year of your university to showcase your capability.
- Join a job in the final year of your university, if possible.
- Do as much as work in your first 3-4 years of your career.
- Be flexible about tech stack, work, and opportunity.
- Gather and gain domain knowledge strongly while working on a project.
- Build genuine relationships with your co-workers.
- Imposter syndrome is natural. Nobody knows all the things.
- Challenge yourself and push your limitations day by day.
- Search for quality work after a certain time of your career.
- Always keep learning.
- Always take ownership and show interest in leadership.
- Raise questions about anything to judge its validity before starting to do it.
- Understand the business impact of your work. Make yourself product-centric.
- Be good at debugging and incident handling.
- Align, align, and align people with data.
- Make a prototype or demo while presenting any ideas.
- Office politics exist; learn to navigate them.
- Act then think. Sometimes too much thinking creates an analysis paralysis situation.
- Soft skills are important. So give proper attention to it.
- Keep attention on work-life balance, especially when you have passed a good amount of time in your career.
- Try hard even if you know you can fail.
- Interview participation is important to judge your ability and upgrade your capability.

---

## Don'ts
- Don't waste time of your university life; utilize it to build skills.
- Don't avoid programming, problem solving, and competitive programming.
- In this competitive job market, don't wait too long to join a job.
- In your early career, don't think too much about job-life balance.
- Don't be too biased about the tech stack.
- Don't just focus on gaining technical knowledge; focus on domain knowledge gathering too.
- Don't break trust and honest relationships with co-workers.
- Don't get stuck in your comfort zone.
- Don't get stuck in flat, regular, and not exciting work.
- Never stop learning.
- Don't hesitate to take leave and break.
- Don't just do what you asked to do; judge its ROI by questioning.
- Don't try to avoid meetings because meetings will be one of the important parts of your job.
- Don't think you are always an individual contributor. You need to be a people manager some day.
- Don't lie while sharing feedback. Your sugar-coated feedback will not be helpful to others.
- Never afraid to fail.
- Don't avoid the importance of soft skills.
- Never stop participating in job interviews, but make sure you don't switch jobs too often.
